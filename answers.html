Diflo's Infection and Immunity Fundamentals

Answer Keys and Explanations for every Board-Style Question, organized by chapter. Question stems and answer choices are printed in each chapter of the book.

Chapter 1: The Language of Infection and Immunity

Question 1
Correct answer: A.
Why the other choices are wrong: Colonization means organisms are present without tissue invasion or symptoms. Infection means the organisms are multiplying in tissue or a normally sterile site. A culture result must be interpreted with the site, specimen quality, and clinical findings.
Question 2
Correct answer: B.
Why the other choices are wrong: IL-1, TNF, IL-6, and prostaglandin E2 act on the hypothalamus and raise the temperature set point. Fever is therefore part of the host response. Some toxins can trigger that response indirectly through immune signaling.
Question 3
Correct answer: A.
Why the other choices are wrong: IL-6 is a major driver of hepatic CRP production. Blocking its receptor can remove an important laboratory warning sign. The patient can still have serious infection even when CRP is low.
Question 4
Correct answer: A.
Why the other choices are wrong: Incubation is the interval from exposure to symptom onset. The prodrome is the early period of nonspecific symptoms. Attack rate and prevalence describe population patterns rather than an individual's incubation.
Question 5
Correct answer: C.
Why the other choices are wrong: Adaptive lymphocytes use rearranged receptors, undergo clonal selection, and form memory. Innate immunity is faster and uses germline-encoded recognition. The two systems cooperate rather than operating independently.
Question 6
Correct answer: D.
Why the other choices are wrong: Burns destroy the epidermal barrier and expose tissue to environmental and colonizing organisms. B-cell, complement, and thymic functions are not directly removed by a burn, although severe injury can later alter immune function.
Question 7
Correct answer: A.
Why the other choices are wrong: Opportunistic organisms cause disease more readily when a defense is missing. The infection pattern often points to the defect. Pneumocystis after T-cell suppression is a classic example.
Question 8
Correct answer: A.
Why the other choices are wrong: Viral injury can impair cilia, disrupt tight junctions, and alter mucus clearance. Bacteria that normally colonize the airway can then reach the lower tract. This is a common example of synergy between infections.
Question 9
Correct answer: A.
Why the other choices are wrong: Virulence depends on microbial factors, host defenses, dose, and the site of infection. The same organism can behave differently in a healthy host and an immunocompromised one. Virulence is not a fixed label.
Question 10
Correct answer: D.
Why the other choices are wrong: Viral injury can impair airway defenses and permit bacterial superinfection. A biphasic course should prompt reassessment. Clinical deterioration does not automatically mean the original treatment failed.

Question 9

Question 10

Chapter 3: Barriers and Resident Defenses

Question 6

Question 7

Question 8

Question 9

Question 10

Question 2

Question 5

Question 6

Question 7
Correct answer: C.

Question 8

Question 9

Question 10
Correct answer: A.
Why the other choices are wrong: Abscess centers can be acidic, low in oxygen, and poorly perfused. These conditions favor anaerobes and reduce antibiotic delivery. Drainage is often necessary.

Question 4

Question 6

Question 9

Question 10

Chapter 7: The Complement System

Question 1
Correct answer: D.
Why the other choices are wrong: Terminal complement forms a pore through C5b, C6, C7, C8, and C9. Neisseria species are especially vulnerable to this pathway. Early complement functions may remain intact.
Question 2
Correct answer: A.
Why the other choices are wrong: C3b is the major opsonin and is recognized by complement receptors on phagocytes. C3a and C5a recruit inflammatory cells. C9 forms part of the membrane attack complex.
Question 3
Correct answer: B.
Why the other choices are wrong: Mannose-binding lectin and ficolins initiate the lectin pathway. Associated proteases cleave C4 and C2. The pathway then converges with the classical pathway.
Question 4
Correct answer: B.
Why the other choices are wrong: Host cells carry regulators such as factor H, CD46, CD55, and CD59. Many microbes lack these protections. The alternative pathway amplifies C3b where regulation is weak.
Question 5
Correct answer: B.
Why the other choices are wrong: C1 inhibitor regulates several proteases, including complement and contact pathway components. Loss leads to excess bradykinin and recurrent angioedema. Urticaria is usually absent.
Question 6
Correct answer: D.
Why the other choices are wrong: C3a and C5a increase vascular permeability and recruit inflammatory cells. C5a is a powerful neutrophil chemoattractant. C5b and C6 participate in the terminal pathway.
Question 7
Correct answer: C.
Why the other choices are wrong: PNH cells lack glycosylphosphatidylinositol-anchored proteins, including CD55 and CD59. Without these regulators, complement damages red cells. The result is intravascular hemolysis.
Question 8
Correct answer: B.
Why the other choices are wrong: CH50 measures classical pathway hemolytic activity. A low result can reflect a component deficiency or consumption. The AH50 test assesses the alternative pathway.
Question 9
Correct answer: D.
Why the other choices are wrong: C5 blockade prevents membrane attack complex formation. Terminal complement is important for defense against Neisseria. Vaccination and urgent evaluation of fever are essential.
Question 10
Correct answer: B.
Why the other choices are wrong: Complement-coated antigen can engage CD21 on B cells and lower the activation threshold. Follicular dendritic cells retain complement-coated antigen. Complement therefore links innate recognition to adaptive antibody responses.

Question 1

Question 2

Question 4

Question 5

Question 6
Correct answer: C.

Question 7
Correct answer: D.

Question 8
Correct answer: B.

Question 9
Correct answer: C.
Why the other choices are wrong: HLA polymorphism changes which peptides can be displayed. A pathogen that escapes one person's alleles may be visible to another person's. This broadens population-level immune coverage.
Question 10
Correct answer: B.
Why the other choices are wrong: Abacavir can bind within HLA-B*57:01 and alter the self-peptide repertoire. Carrier T cells may then attack tissues and cause hypersensitivity. Screening reduces a serious preventable reaction.

Question 2

Question 5

Question 6

Question 7

Question 8

Question 9

Question 10

Why the other choices are wrong: Avidity combines multiple binding interactions and can be high for multimeric antibodies such as IgM. Affinity refers to one binding site and one epitope. Both influence protection.
